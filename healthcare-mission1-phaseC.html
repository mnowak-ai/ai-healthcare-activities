Step 01 — Your Operational Findings

What You Discovered at Lakeside

This is a summary of the operational problems you found during Phase A and Phase B. Read through it — you will be using this exact information to create your AI analysis prompt in the next steps.

Top Workflow Problems
Scheduling double-bookings from two unsynchronized calendar systems
Documentation backlog — nurses charting from memory hours after patient visits
Front desk overload — one person managing all check-in, phones, and insurance
No formal process for patient callbacks or lab result communication
Departments Most Affected
Front Desk — single point of failure, no backup system
Nursing — documentation burden reducing patient care time
Scheduling — manual workarounds creating repeated errors
Patients — experiencing downstream effects of all the above
Staff Concerns Identified
Nurses writing notes from memory 3–6 hours after patient visits
Physician seeing patients with incomplete or missing chart records
3 near-miss medication errors flagged this year — all documentation-related
Staff morale declining — experienced employees considering leaving
Suspected Root Causes
Technology systems that do not talk to each other
No ownership of critical tasks like lab results and patient callbacks
No staffing backup plan when employees are absent
No operational dashboard — manager tracks everything manually
Why does this matter? AI tools are only as good as the information you give them. A clear, organized summary like this will produce much better AI analysis. Professionals call this principle: "garbage in, garbage out."

Step 04 — Evaluate the AI Response

Read the AI Response Critically

Not every AI recommendation is useful or safe to act on. Before you bring the analysis back here, use this guide while you read the AI's response. Look for signs of quality — and look out for red flags.

✅ Signs of Good AI Analysis
Connects problems across departments
Recommends process improvements, not just new software
Suggests human review before any major change
Identifies root causes, not just symptoms
Is realistic about what AI can and cannot do
Raises questions leadership should investigate
Prioritizes patient safety and staff wellbeing
⚠️ Red Flags to Question
Suggests replacing healthcare staff with AI
Recommends expensive software before fixing processes
Sounds confident but ignores operational reality
Gives vague advice like "implement a better system"
Ignores the human impact of making changes
Assumes AI can make clinical decisions
Jumps to action before understanding root cause
Remember: AI is a thinking tool, not a decision maker. It can help you organize ideas and spot patterns — but a human must review every recommendation before anything is acted on in a real healthcare setting.
