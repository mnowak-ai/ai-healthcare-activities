▸ Workflow Analysis

Where Are the Bottlenecks?

Select a workflow area to explore what's going wrong — and start thinking about why.

📅

Scheduling Breakdown

WORKFLOW AREA 01

WHY IT'S BROKEN

Appointments are tracked in a shared spreadsheet. Two people can book the same slot. Urgent patients wait weeks for openings that don't really exist.

HOW IT AFFECTS PATIENTS

Patients show up for appointments that were double-booked. They leave without care. Some stop trying to schedule at all.

🤔 START THINKING…

Could a smarter system detect open slots and fill them automatically? Who should still make the final scheduling decision?

📋

Documentation Overload

WORKFLOW AREA 02

WHY IT'S BROKEN

Clinicians type notes from memory after every visit. The same facts — name, DOB, symptoms — are re-entered into three different systems. Notes pile up overnight.

HOW IT AFFECTS PATIENTS

Late notes mean billing is delayed. If a patient returns before the note is done, the provider is flying blind — they can't see what happened last visit.

🤔 START THINKING…

Could AI help draft notes from a visit? What information should only a licensed clinician enter or approve? Where does accuracy matter most?

💬

Communication Gaps

WORKFLOW AREA 03

WHY IT'S BROKEN

Appointment reminders are made by hand. Callbacks sit in voicemail. Staff use different apps to message each other. Nothing connects to patient records.

HOW IT AFFECTS PATIENTS

Patients forget appointments. They don't get reminders in their language. Test results sit unsent for days. Patients feel ignored and unsafe.

🤔 START THINKING…

What messages could be sent automatically? What messages — like test results or bad news — should only come from a human?

🏥

Front Desk Overload

WORKFLOW AREA 04

WHY IT'S BROKEN

3 staff handle everything: phones, walk-ins, check-ins, and billing questions — all at once. Many calls are simple questions that don't need a human to answer.

HOW IT AFFECTS PATIENTS

Patients wait on hold. Real emergencies get mixed in with routine calls. Staff are too burned out to give everyone the attention they deserve.

🤔 START THINKING…

Which questions could a self-service tool answer? What requires empathy and judgment? How do you keep patients from feeling like just a number?

▸ Critical Thinking

Can AI Help? Start Thinking.

Tap each card to explore the question. There are no wrong answers yet — just good thinking.

🔁

Where could AI reduce repetitive work?

Think about tasks that happen the same way every time — the ones staff do dozens of times a day without much variation.

Consider: Sending the same appointment reminder. Entering the same address into three systems. Answering "What are your hours?" for the 40th time today. Repetitive = a strong signal AI might help.

🧠

What tasks still require human judgment?

Think about decisions where getting it wrong could hurt someone — where context, emotion, and expertise matter most.

Consider: Deciding if symptoms are an emergency. Delivering a difficult diagnosis. Interpreting a complex test result. AI can support — but never replace — human training and accountability.

▸ tap to explore
❤️

Which problems affect patient experience most?

Think from the patient's point of view. They don't see the back office — they feel the wait, the confusion, and the silence when no one calls back.

Consider: Long waits make patients feel unimportant. Missed reminders make them feel forgotten. No callbacks make them feel unsafe. The emotional impact of poor operations affects health outcomes.

▸ tap to explore
🚫

What should AI NEVER handle alone?

Some decisions are too important, too personal, or too risky to automate. Think about what happens when AI gets it wrong in a healthcare setting.

Consider: Diagnosing a patient. Deciding who gets an urgent appointment. Communicating serious news. Handling a mental health crisis. A trained human must always review and take responsibility for these moments.
